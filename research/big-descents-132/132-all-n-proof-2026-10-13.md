# A finite certificate for all-index real-rootedness of B132

Owner: academic RealRooted theory lane. Related issue: #1623.
Status: complete computer-assisted mathematical proof for every n. The analytic
tail n>=100 passed independent exact verification, and all finite rows below
100 passed exact distinct-root counting. No Lean verification is claimed.

## Statement

Let B_n(t) enumerate permutations pi of {1,...,n} avoiding 132, by the
number of indices i with pi_i>pi_(i+1)+1. Put B_0=1. Equivalently, for
n>=1 use the coefficient polynomials of the unique formal series V in z
with zero constant term satisfying

    (1-2z+(1-t)z^2)V = z+t*z*(1-(1-t)z)*V^2.

We prove: every B_n has only simple strictly negative roots.
The constant cases B_0=B_1=1 and B_2=2 satisfy this vacuously. For n>=1,
the degree is d_n=floor(2(n-1)/3). The first nonconstant examples are
B_3=3+2t, B_4=4+9t+t^2 and B_5=5+25t+12t^2.

The combinatorial generating-function identity and the positive leading
coefficient/degree formula are the established starting point. Formalizing
the recurrence family and identifying it with the permutation enumerator
are separate obligations; an explicit model-identity boundary does not
permit assuming real-rootedness or any analytic comparison.

## Exact analytic reduction

For s>1, let a,b,lambda,conj(lambda) be the roots of

    W_s(w)=w^4-4w^3+6s*w^2-4s^2*w+s^2,

ordered by 0<a<1<b<s and Im(lambda)>0. Write R=|lambda|>b and
theta=arg(lambda). The quartic geometry gives 0<theta<2pi/3. Define

    c(u)=sqrt(u-a/lambda)*sqrt(u-b/lambda)*sqrt(u-conj(lambda)/lambda),
    H(u)=c(u)/c(1)*(lambda-s)/(u*lambda-s),
    I_n=integral_0^1 u^(n-1)*sqrt(1-u)*H(u) du,
    kappa=-c(1)*lambda^2/[4sqrt(pi)*(s-1)*(lambda-s)],
    T_n=1/[2pi*(s-1)] integral_a^b
          x^(n-1)*sqrt((x-a)*(b-x))*|x-lambda|/(s-x) dx,
    A_n=(4/sqrt(pi))*|kappa|*R^n*Re(I_n).

The square roots in c are principal. Their arguments avoid the branch
cut on 0<=u<=1; in particular H is continuous and H(1)=1. The exact
contour identity, with orientations and removable pole checked, is

    B_n(1-s)=T_n+(4/sqrt(pi))*Re(kappa*lambda^n*I_n), n>=1.       (1)

Its proof is in `132-uniform-oscillation-2026-10-13.md`, section
"An exact radial integral". We need this exact section, not its later
asymptotic arguments. Always T_n>0.

The continuous normalized phase is

    phi=(arg(lambda-a)+arg(lambda-b)+theta)/2-arg(lambda-s)-3pi/4,
    Phi_n=n*theta+phi.

All arguments displayed here are upper-half-plane principal arguments.
We have exp(i*phi)=kappa/|kappa|, -1/2<dphi/dtheta<1/2, and

    Phi_n(1+)=-5pi/6,
    Phi_n(infinity)=2(n-1)pi/3=(d_n+ell_n/3)pi,
    ell_n=2n-3d_n-2 in {0,1,2}.

These identities, including the phase sign, are proved in the first
section of `132-phase-count-matching-2026-10-13.md`. Phi_n is strictly
increasing for n>=1. At its unique finite grid points Phi_n=m*pi,
equation (1) becomes

    B_n(1-s)=T_n+(-1)^m*A_n.                                  (2)

Thus 0<T_n<A_n gives alternating nonzero signs. If ell_n>0, the
grid m=0,...,d_n gives d_n distinct strictly negative roots. If ell_n=0,
the finite grid stops at d_n-1; the positive leading coefficient gives
the final far-left sign (-1)^d_n and the final root. The degree bound
then proves splitting and simplicity, with no possible additional roots.

## Why the compact certificate proves an infinite tail

The root-angle inequality pi/6<arg(lambda-a)<2pi/3 gives

    d/dx [(x-a)|x-lambda|^2]
      =3*(x-(a+2Re(lambda))/3)^2
          +Im(lambda)^2-(Re(lambda)-a)^2/3 >0.

Using s-x>=b-x in T_n and the beta ratio
Beta(n,1/2)/Beta(n,3/2)=2n+1, we obtain, whenever Re(H(u))>h0>0,

    T_n/A_n <= D*(2n+1)*rho^n,
    rho=b/R,
    D=|b-lambda|sqrt(b-a)|lambda-s|/[2h0|c(1)|R^2 sqrt(b)].     (3)

Put r=R^2, v=Im(lambda), and D_x=|lambda-x|^2. Then

    D^4=D_b*(b-a)^2*D_s^2/[64h0^4*D_a*v^2*r*b^2].             (4)

The compact artifact proves (3) is less than one uniformly by exact
interval arithmetic. It does not numerically integrate T_n or I_n.

We use P_q(z)=z^4+6qz^2+(4-4q^3)z-3q^2, with real roots alpha<0<beta.
The original roots are a=1+q*alpha, b=1+q*beta, lambda=1+q*Z, where

    Re Z=-(alpha+beta)/2,
    (Im Z)^2=6q+3alpha^2/4+alpha*beta/2+3beta^2/4.

For each rational q interval, root brackets are checked by strict signs
at both ends for every q in that interval. With tau=(1-u)/q, write

    da=Z-alpha, db=Z-beta, dc=2i*Im Z, ds=Z-q^2.
    H(u)^2=(da-lambda*tau)*(db-lambda*tau)*(dc-lambda*tau)*ds^2
            /[da*db*dc*(ds-lambda*tau)^2].                    (5)

Every tau interval in [0,1/q_lower] is covered. If H^2=X+iY, the
strict rational inequality

    E=Y^2+4h0^2*X-4h0^4>0                                   (6)

implies |Re H|>h0. This follows by factoring E as
(|H^2|+X-2h0^2)(|H^2|-X+2h0^2). The second factor is positive.
Continuity and H(1)=1 fix the positive sign on the full interval.

Finally, let d4 bound (4) above and let 0<rho_*<1 bound rho above.
For integers n>=100, (2n+1)rho_*^n takes its maximum at

    m=max(100,ceil((3rho_*-1)/(2(1-rho_*)))).

This follows by dividing successive terms. The certificate checks the
single exact rational inequality

    d4*(2m+1)^4*rho_*^(4m)<1.                                (7)

An equality in the adjacent ratio only creates two equal maxima.
Thus the argument covers every integer n>=100, including indices
larger than any explicitly computed polynomial row.
